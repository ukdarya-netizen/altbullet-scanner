name: Impulse Buy runner exit policy
description: Impulse Buy runner exit policy and its accounting/lifecycle constraints.
---

New long Impulse Buy positions freeze a core/runner exit policy: the core follows standard management and closes at its confirmed trailing exit; after core close, the runner's emergency stop is breakeven at the immutable original entry, and a 30-percentage-point trailing gap activates after the total peak reaches +30%.

**Why:** Production replay showed that replacing early protection for the entire position worsened the full cohort. Keeping the existing core exit while retaining a bounded 20% runner improved the unambiguous trailing-exit cohort. A later minute-candle replay of all eight runners with a closed core found that the two negative completed runners would have closed at breakeven, while none of the three positive completed or three still-open runners revisited entry after core close. The completed canonical runner sum would have improved from +221.32% to +239.32%. The cohort is small, so this supports downside protection rather than broad trailing retuning. A production race once let core close overtake a claimed first target, so the lower milestone arrived much later; runner close also repeatedly failed because a computed SQL status was inferred as text instead of the database enum.

**How to apply:** The breakeven emergency floor applies to every open runner after core close, including already-open legacy-policy runners; it does not alter positions before the core closes. Core and runner settlements are separate durable, exactly-once lifecycle events. Canonical final PnL and status are always the pure runner return from the immutable original entry to runner exit; there is no 80/20 weighted canonical accounting. Core and runner values may be retained as clearly labelled component telemetry only and must never affect totals or status. Generic close/cancel/reclassification claims must atomically exclude an open runner. Resolve the take-profit image after the atomic claim from the persisted runner PnL so a durable trailing floor and stale worker input cannot disagree with the media. A runner close must require durable core PnL, derive its stop from the highest persisted peak in the same atomic claim, and never lower that peak or stop when a stale worker arrives. Keep the trailing stop NULL while trailing is unarmed; the emergency floor remains original entry. After activation, the 30-point gap is subtracted in percentage points and cannot fall below breakeven. After core close, the open runner continues the position's existing monotonic milestone stream. Telegram milestone recovery may continue independently, but it must never defer a runner stop; final close keeps pending milestone state and owns a stable outbox event. Startup recovery checks closed 1m candles after core close or durable trailing-stop arming so a crossing during downtime or delivery delay cannot be lost after price rebounds. SQL expressions that calculate a tracked status must explicitly cast to the database enum. Price-derived startup repairs correct only rows with complete immutable prices: they must raise a stale runner exit to the stricter of the peak-derived floor and a valid persisted stop before recalculating PnL. Audits distinguish the peak-earned floor from the effective persisted floor. If a repair changes the user-visible canonical PnL, a post-commit reconciler must enqueue one explicit correction through the normal recipient-level outbox under a versioned deterministic event key; fixing the row alone is incomplete. Ambiguous historical rows are quarantined without blocking startup or receiving fabricated PnL.

**Rule:** After 120 elapsed hours from the original opening, an open runner with a closed core closes with its earned trailing protection as TAKE PROFIT, or at its entry-anchored emergency BE as `bu_stop` when trailing has not armed. An armed lifetime settlement may be above the earned floor; protection is a minimum, not a requirement to replace a higher observed mark with the floor. This is a close, not merely a stop adjustment. A separately requested one-time Take Profit for runners already overdue at the earlier policy change remains separate.

**Why:** The owner previously required observed-price expiry for runners, then explicitly replaced that rule with a universal protected-stop/trailing lifetime policy before many positions reached expiry. Age still must not restart when the core closes.

**How to apply:** Check the immutable original opening time in fast-cycle and startup recovery. At timeout retain the runner's durable trailing floor or emergency BE; canonical PnL remains runner-only. In replays, verify the atomic settlement contract, not just the caller's observed price: unarmed lifetime closes use BE, while armed closes preserve a higher observed mark above the protected floor. Neither is evidence of exchange execution. Preserve the age-close identity and exclude such rows from historical price-floor repair, which would otherwise override a deliberate lifetime classification. Keep the explicitly requested pre-existing overdue Take Profit separate and idempotent. Do not retroactively reclassify historical expires.
